# Taste profile

A worked example with invented preferences. Yours will differ on every line. What matters is the
level of detail, because a vague profile produces vague picks.

Anything marked `(inferred)` was guessed from the library rather than said out loud, so it is fair
game to correct.

## Language lanes

Ranked, widest to narrowest:

**English = Korean > Japanese > Spanish > everything else**

Subtitles are fine and not a barrier. Dubbed audio is a hard no, it flattens the performance.

## The centre of gravity

Character driven crime and slow burn thrillers. Films where the plot is an excuse to watch people
under pressure. *Heat*, *Memories of Murder*, *Zodiac* are the shape of it.

Not chosen by cast or director. Recognising a name is not the same as picking by it. One exception:
anything by Bong Joon-ho gets watched on sight, and that is a marker for tonally slippery films that
change genre halfway, not a licence to start pitching other directors.

## What gets walked out of

- **Comedy where the premise is the whole joke.** Runs out by minute forty.
- **Anything sold on spectacle alone.** Big budget, thin script. The 1 out of 10 ratings in the
  library are all this pattern. `(inferred, worth confirming)`
- **Horror that is only jump scares.** Dread is fine, startle is not.

## Rewatches

*Heat*, *The Thing*, *Before Sunset*. Three completely different films. The common thread is that
all three hold up when you already know what happens, which is the real test.

## Weeknight against weekend

**Weeknight, low energy.** Under a hundred minutes. Something already familiar, or a single episode
of something light. Nothing that needs subtitles when tired. `(inferred from watch times)`

**Weekend, full attention.** Three hours is fine. Subtitles are fine. This is where the ambitious
and difficult picks go.

## Series appetite

Will start a three season show. Five seasons needs a genuine reason. Anything with a fifty episode
back catalogue is a no regardless of quality, unless it is genuinely self contained per episode.

Currently part way through two shows and unlikely to finish either. Do not suggest starting a third
until one of those is closed out.

## Standing notes

- No spoilers, ever, including "it gets better in season two" style hints about structure.
- Runtime matters more than genre on a weeknight. Lead with it.
- Rating gap is real: about eighty titles rated out of two thousand watched. Do not read an unrated
  title as a dislike.
